THE BRIDGE

You just faced a problem that democracies deal with all the time.

What happens when what is good for YOU and what is good for EVERYONE are not the same?

Democracy makes two promises that can sometimes collide.

  • Protect each person's freedom

  • Make decisions for the good of everyone

The hard part is deciding where one should end and the other should begin.

Perfect freedom

How much of your life should really belong to you?

Your beliefs. Your opinions. Your relationships. What you do in private. Most people believe there are parts of life where the government should have very little say.

CLASSICAL LIBERALISM is the belief that individual rights and freedom should be protected from too much government control.

But freedom gets complicated. Laws always limit someone's choices.

The real question is how much control should government have over the way you live your own life?

EQ: How can democracy protect individual rights while promoting the common good?

COMMON GOOD

But there’s another side.

Sometimes what you choose does not affect only you. Driving drunk can kill someone. Dumping toxic waste can poison a community. Not vaccinating a child. One person's decision can create consequences for many others.

CLASSICAL REPUBLICANISM is the belief that citizens should work together for the good of the whole community.

That can mean accepting rules or limits you personally do not want because other people are affected too.

So when should your freedom give way to what is best for everyone?

A New World

In 1831, ALEXIS DE TOCQUEVILLE was just 25 years old when he traveled from France to the United States. America was still a young democracy, and Tocqueville wanted to see what happened when ordinary people had real political power.

He traveled across the country, talked with Americans, and turned his observations into a famous book called Democracy in America.

Tocqueville liked much of what he saw. Giving ordinary people political power could make democracy stronger.

But he also noticed what could happen when the people became the ones in control.

Equality of conditions

Tocqueville came from France, where wealth and family background could decide your place in society before you were even born. America looked different to him.

More ordinary people could own property, participate in their communities, vote, and expect their voices to matter.

Tocqueville called this EQUALITY OF CONDITIONS. He did not mean everyone was actually equal.

Women, enslaved people, Native Americans, and many free Black Americans were still largely excluded.

America was becoming more democratic, but democracy still did not include everyone.

tyranny of the majority

But Tocqueville noticed a problem. Giving the people power also gives the majority power.

Imagine 70% of voters support a new law and 30% oppose it. The law passes. That is majority rule.

Tocqueville called this TYRANNY OF THE MAJORITY. It happens when the majority uses its power in ways that threaten the rights of a smaller group.

Tocqueville believed the majority should have the power to govern, but not the power to do whatever it wants. Majority rule must stop when it becomes unjust or violates the rights of others.

EQ: How can democracy protect individual rights while promoting the common good?

TOCQUEVILLE'S WARNING

Tocqueville had uncovered a problem inside democracy itself. The people need power to make decisions for the COMMON GOOD, but the majority cannot have unlimited power over everyone else.

INDIVIDUAL RIGHTS still need protection, especially when someone's beliefs, choices, or opinions are unpopular.

CLASSICAL REPUBLICANISM asks us to think about the community. CLASSICAL LIBERALISM protects individual freedom. Democracy needs both, which leaves one difficult question.

When should the COMMON GOOD outweigh INDIVIDUAL RIGHTS, and when should RIGHTS be protected?

LESSON RECAP

Democracy works best when it gives people power to pursue the COMMON GOOD while protecting INDIVIDUAL RIGHTS from too much government or majority control.

• CLASSICAL LIBERALISM protects individual freedom.
• CLASSICAL REPUBLICANISM focuses on the good of the community.
• Democracy gives ordinary people the power to make decisions together.
• TOCQUEVILLE warned that majority power can threaten individual rights.
